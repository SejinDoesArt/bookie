1) What Counts as a “Signal”

First thing’s first: a signal isn’t just a hunch. It’s a snapshot—fast, precise, and based on a stream of high-fidelity data. The engine tracks recent action to stay ahead of the curve.

  • Core Data: Rolling ~1-hour window; downsampled ticks (~2s) and 1-minute bars.
  • Trend: Separation of fast/slow EMAs mapped to a 0–100 score.
  • Impulse: Acceleration via EMA-slope delta, slightly short-biased.
  • Momentum: 25–45s log return normalized by recent vol.
  • Volatility: Z-score of minute returns → calm / normal / hot.
  • Liquidity (liqScore): Percentile of activity; hard veto if too low.
  • Risk Context: Levels, breakouts/breakdowns, “decision points.”
  • Final Score: Base score adjusted for risk context and OB/OS → generalScore (0–100).

2) The Quality Funnel

A high score is a start, not a ticket. Every candidate runs a gauntlet; only the best reach you.

  1. Vetoes: Liquidity too low, contradictions, “Storm Rider,” or cooldown → tossed.
  2. Arming: Momentum/EMA/Impulse agree → becomes a candidate.
  3. Confirmation: Must cross a dynamic target within ~90–150s.
  4. Tiering: Strength by distance from 50: strong (≥22), moderate (≥12).
  5. Broadcast: Only strong signals hit the durable outbox → Telegram.

3) Picking a Direction (Long vs. Short)

  • Confidence Check: Calibrated win probabilities; must clear ~58% threshold.
  • Alignment & Risk: Multi-TF alignment; don’t long into a breakdown. Huge impulse can override minor risk.
  • Bootstrap Mode: On fresh resets, thresholds relax slightly to learn—still only strong signals broadcast.
  • Storm Rider Veto: Market-wide chaos (BTC/ETH impulse) blocks longs in a crash.

4) How the System Learns & Improves

The engine is a learning machine with two loops:

A) Forward-Tested Signals → Decile Calibration

Each fired signal is tracked for one hour; results bucketed by coin, action, and score decile build a probability map.

B) Historical Analysis → Pattern Database

Back-simulates the firing logic on history and records outcomes plus context (impulse, momentum, vol label, etc.). Similar past patterns can veto a weak look-alike.

5) Why It Drops More Bad Signals Over Time

  • Confidence Gating: Better calibration → weak setups fail early.
  • Pattern Matching: Contextual filter vetoes known traps.
  • Strict Guardrails: OB/OS caps and Storm Rider remain hard limits.

6) End-to-End Trace: The Perfect Play

  1. Compute features.
  2. Veto Check passes.
  3. Confidence & Alignment clear.
  4. Arming sets target & window.
  5. Confirmation hits within window.
  6. Tiering & Queuing → strong only.
  7. Broadcast via Telegram.
  8. Learn outcomes fold back in.

Clean, smart, and always playing the odds. Now, let’s go find a winner.
